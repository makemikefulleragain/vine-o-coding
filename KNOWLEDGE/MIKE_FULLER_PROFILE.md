## Who He Is

Mike Fuller is a Perth-based Quality Assurance, Improvement, and Innovation specialist with over 30 years of professional experience. He is the founder and principal of **Kamunity Consulting** and the sole builder of the **Kamunity ecosystem** — a constitutional AI infrastructure project for community organisations.

He is Scottish by origin and education, now rooted in Perth, WA on Whadjuk Noongar boodja. His entry point to all of this was unexpectedly human: he started in charity street fundraising — describing himself as a **"chugger"** — and rose to *Regional Best Practice Manager* at Fruitful On-Street Fundraising in Edinburgh. That experience gave him a foundational insight — *resources, passion, and money are available on any high street, if you talk to the right people.* Every Kamunity tool is downstream of that lesson. His career spans brewing, mining, higher education, and community services across three continents, converging into a single mission: make the invisible visible in communities that corporate tech leaves behind.

He is building Kamunity solo, with AI assistance, pre-revenue, while planning his wedding and running a live consulting engagement (WALGA). Burnout risk is a named and tracked concern in his own operational system.

## The Philosophy Behind the Work

Several consistent threads run through 30+ years of career and across both the consulting practice and the Kamunity project:

### 1. "Make the Invisible Visible"
This is not just a tagline — it is a methodology. Whether it is waste in a manufacturing process (Lean), unspoken needs in a community org (human-centred design), or data flows through AI tools (sovereignty audit), Mike's work is about surfacing what is hidden and acting on it.

### 2. Simple Solutions to Complex Problems
> *"The context is complex and challenging, but the solutions can still be simple, effective, and affordable."*

This principle is baked into every Kamunity tool. The Priya Test (does this serve a 12-person NFP coordinator with 15 minutes?) is a direct expression of this.

### 3. Constitutional Governance
The Kamunity Constitution (11 inviolable principles) is the most explicit expression of his values system made operational. Notably:
- Principle 1: Real users, real problems (not technical elegance)
- Principle 4: Evidence changes the plan (data beats assumptions)
- Principle 5: Data sovereignty (community orgs own their data)
- Principle 8: Cultural safety (Whadjuk Noongar boodja, Noongar review required)
- Principle 10: Ontological honesty (Kai is an AI, never a counsellor)

### 4. Post-Capitalist Infrastructure
Kamunity is explicitly described as "post-capitalist community infrastructure." This positions it outside the venture capital and platform economy frame — not a product you sign up for hoping pricing doesn't change, but a community commons. The NLnet grant (€35K, NGI Zero Commons fund) and the planned cooperative legal structure reflect this.

### 5. The Priya Test as Design Constraint
Everything is evaluated against whether it serves Priya — the operations coordinator at a 12-person NFP who has 15 minutes, no tech background, and a CEO asking "should we be using AI?" This is a specific, humanised, tested design constraint — not an abstract "user story."

### 6. Post-Modern Structuralism and Intellectual Heritage
Fuller describes himself as a **"post-modern structuralist"** — all models are wrong, but some elements are useful. His primary intellectual heritage is the post-WWII quality movement: **W. Edwards Deming, Joseph Juran, Peter Drucker**. He explicitly positions himself against McKinsey-style consultancies as extractive rather than transformative — favouring ethical, worker-inclusive systems.

### 7. "Enshittification" as Diagnostic Lens
He uses the term **"enshittification"** to name the gradual decline of quality in systems optimised purely for short-term profit. Rebranding Agile, Lean, or Six Sigma without genuine change is, in his framing, a form of enshittification — extracting efficiency without creating value.

### 8. Technology Agnosticism
Tools are irrelevant. Intent is everything. Whether an organisation is using Teams, Trello, Zapier, or AI, the question is whether it fits *their* process — not whether the process can be reshaped to fit the software. Kamunity's constitutional-first design reflects this: the tech stack serves the values, not the other way around.
