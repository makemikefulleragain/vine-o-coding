## A. Infoxchange 2025 Report — Key Findings (Previously Inaccessible)

Source: Funding Centre article by Nick Place (11 Nov 2025), summarising the Infoxchange 2025 Digital Technology in the Not-for-Profit Sector Report (10th edition, 824 organisations surveyed across AU/NZ).

### Core Statistics
- **67% of organisations** use generative AI (mostly content creation) — corrects the 76% figure from the Liferay article which was from a different sample
- **Only 14% have an AI policy or guidelines** — 50% intend to create one
- **Only 23% have a documented cybersecurity plan**
- **Fewer than half of orgs with 150 staff or less** have effective info security risk management
- **Only 20% of volunteer-led organisations** use multi-factor authentication
- **87% use cloud-based collaboration tools** (up from 24% a decade ago)
- **Use of AI has doubled** year-on-year

### Key Quotes
- David Spriggs (Infoxchange CEO): "Digital technology and AI has the potential to improve efficiencies and outcomes for an over-burdened and resource-stretched sector, but we're seeing a major gap in the capability levels that could help close inequality gaps in our society."
- "Australia needs a unified, national approach to digital capability improvement in the not-for-profit sector."
- Sophie Souchon (Infoxchange digital transformation manager): Budgets/funding are top priority, "with a close second being access to affordable and skilled technical resources."
- David Crosbie (CCA CEO): "Internally it's often a difficult decision to decide how much to invest in IT as compared to direct services or other needs."
- Charities Minister Andrew Leigh: "We need to be intentional and ethical in how we use it."

### What Digitally Advanced NFPs Look Like
The report highlighted orgs where strong tech use led to: "evidence-based findings through improved data and analytics, improved content, streamlined referrals, stronger evaluations through technology co-designed with those with lived experience, and more vigilant cyber security tools to protect sensitive data."

### Top Barriers to AI Adoption
Data security, ethics, sovereignty and privacy — not cost or complexity.

### Relevance to Kamunity
- Confirms capacity gap finding from I02 with primary data (824 orgs)
- The barrier framing (security, ethics, sovereignty, privacy) aligns directly with the Constitution's principles
- "Co-designed with those with lived experience" — validates the listen-first approach
- The tension between "invest in IT vs direct services" is real and present in the sector
- Volunteer-led orgs are the most vulnerable (20% MFA adoption) — these are the orgs closest to Kamunity's target communities

## B. AIATSIS Code of Ethics for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Research

### The Four Principles
1. **Indigenous self-determination** — Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have the right to determine their own research priorities, control the research process, and own the outcomes
2. **Indigenous leadership** — Research must be led by, or in genuine partnership with, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples
3. **Impact and value** — Research must deliver tangible benefits to the communities involved
4. **Sustainability and accountability** — Research relationships must be sustained beyond the project, with ongoing accountability to communities

### Key Requirements
- All research involving Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples requires ethics review and approval BEFORE the project begins
- The Code ensures "meaningful engagement and reciprocity between the researcher and the individuals and/or communities involved"
- Supersedes the older GERAIS guidelines (2012)

### Relevance to Kamunity
- **Non-negotiable prerequisite** for any engagement with Aboriginal community-controlled organisations
- Principle 1 (self-determination) maps directly to Constitution's Sovereignty First
- Principle 4 (sustainability and accountability) means: don't start a conversation you can't continue
- The community listening package must NOT include Aboriginal community engagement without first establishing an appropriate relationship pathway — this is not something the engine can design unilaterally
- **Recommendation:** Any Aboriginal community engagement should be led by Aboriginal people, not designed by an AI engine. The listening package should acknowledge this boundary explicitly.

## C. Australian Neighbourhood Houses & Community Centres

### What They Are (ANHCA)
- **1,000+ Neighbourhood Houses and Community Centres** nationally, represented through 6 state sector peaks
- Community-based, locally governed
- Focus: social connection, community development, education, recreation
- "Bring people together to connect, learn and contribute in their local community"

### Government Investment
- Albanese Government investing $989,000 to bolster neighbourhood houses and centres
- Goal: better access to essential government services + building social connections

### What They Already Do Well (Strengths-Based Evidence)
- Physical spaces for community coordination already exist
- Place-based, locally responsive, community-governed
- Bridge between government services and community
- Informal coordination happens through these spaces (knowledge sharing, referrals, peer support)
- Run by and for their local communities

### Relevance to Kamunity
- These are the Australian equivalent of what Hylo tries to enable digitally — but they already exist physically
- The "Coordination Support Gap" may actually be: "what digital complement do these already-coordinating community spaces need?"
- WA has its own network — potential listening target through WALGA/LGA connections
- The strengths-based reframe starts here: communities ARE coordinating, through physical spaces and relationships. The question is where that breaks down.
