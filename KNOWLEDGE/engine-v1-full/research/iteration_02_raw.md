## A. Hylo Deep-Dive

### A1. What Hylo Is
- Open-source, non-profit, community-led "prosocial coordination platform for purpose-driven groups"
- Built for regenerative agriculture, community resilience, peer learning, equity, inclusive capital, place-based organizing
- Unique features: **nested groups** and **cross-group collaboration** enabling emergent cooperation between groups
- Bioregional organizing focus — aligns human activities with natural ecosystems
- Name from "hylozoism" (everything is alive) — holistic, interconnected philosophy

### A2. Governance & Structure
- **Stewardship:** Terran Collective (California Bay Area community of care/practice)
- **Legal:** Hylo Foundation is a fiscally-sponsored 501(c)(3)
- **Co-steward:** Holo Ltd (makers of Holochain) provides technology under Apache License 2.0
- **Governance model:** Sociocracy (dynamic governance) with General Circle, Purpose Circle, Product Circle, Dev Circle, Community Circle
- **Transition in progress:** Assembling advisory board, establishing independent entity, aiming to enshrine Hylo as "technology commons with participatory governance model that formally includes stakeholder voices"
- **Open finances:** Transparent via Open Collective

### A3. Funding & Sustainability
- Donation-driven via Open Collective
- ~$85K annual budget (estimated from Open Collective data)
- 102 financial contributors
- Partners: OpenTEAM, Planetary Health Alliance, Prosocial.World, Holo
- **Risk signal:** Small donation base + small team (5-6 people) = sustainability question mark
- No evidence of earned revenue, government grants, or institutional funding

### A4. Theory of Change & Trust
- **Core belief:** "Deep trust, strong relationships, and a collaborative culture lead to the most impactful groups"
- **Sequencing:** "Before people can successfully engage in complex coordination like making decisions together about money, they need to know each other, and they need to trust each other"
- **Tech's proper place:** "To enhance in-person experiences and strengthen face-to-face relationships, not replace them"
- **Self-awareness:** "Successful coordination takes more than technology — it requires deep trust, strong relationships, and a lot of human care"
- **Capacity building built in:** "We are community leaders and prosocial facilitators that guide our member groups on best practices in community stewardship"

### A5. Team
- Tibet Sprague (Director of Technology) — communitarian technologist, Brown CS graduate
- Clare Politano (Director of Product) — nonprofit communications + software engineering, ProSocial facilitator, specialises in participatory governance
- Tree Willard (Director of Development) — Two Spirit Cherokee citizen, nonprofit leader, antiracist community organizer
- Krisha Subramanian (Community Lead) — holistic wellness, regenerative living
- Aaron Broduer (Director of Design) — interface/systems designer, Holochain ecosystem contributor

### A6. Gaps in Hylo Research
- **No user testimony found** — no community group case studies, no practitioner reviews
- **No Australian deployment evidence** — Bay Area centric
- **No data on active users, group count, or retention** — community health unknown

## B. Australian NFP Digital Divide

### B1. Infoxchange/Liferay Analysis (2025 data)
- **Digital divide widening:** Larger orgs advancing, smaller NFPs struggling with limited resources and fragmented systems
- **Only 1/3 of organisations** report IT systems work well
- **Only 22%** can effectively measure impact of services and outcomes
- **Only 20% of small-to-medium NFPs** have an IT plan and budget (vs 56% of very large orgs)
- **76% use generative AI tools** but foundational infrastructure often missing entirely
- **37% rank data/reporting** as top tech priority (up from 17% prior year)
- **57% lack multi-factor authentication;** only 20% conduct staff cybersecurity training

### B2. Key Quotes
- "There's a dangerous gap between aspiration and capability that's only getting wider" — Paul Towers, Liferay ANZ
- "A sector that understands the importance of digital transformation but lacks the foundational infrastructure and strategic approach to achieve it"

### B3. Infoxchange Digital Transformation Hub
- Delivers digital transformation for hundreds of Australian/NZ/APAC organisations
- Provides practical guides on: technology foundations, information systems, digital marketing, IT management, cyber security, data
- Funded by Lord Mayor's Charitable Foundation
- Offers NFP technology discounts and tailored expert advice
- **Exists as a capacity-building resource already in the Australian ecosystem**

## C. Capacity Building Lens (Cross-cutting)

### C1. Evidence That Capacity Building > New Tools
1. Hylo itself says: "successful coordination takes more than technology"
2. Australian NFPs: aspiration-capability gap is the core problem, not tool availability
3. Only 20% of small-medium NFPs have any IT plan — the gap is strategic, not technological
4. Infoxchange Digital Transformation Hub already exists as capacity support
5. COVID mutual aid (Iteration 01): groups started with simple tools, iterated based on need
6. Reddit practitioners (Iteration 01): "utilitarian UI" of open-source tools makes adoption hard for less-technical members

### C2. Evidence That New/Better Coordination Tools Matter
1. Hylo's nested groups and cross-group collaboration are genuinely unique features not found in mainstream tools
2. 37% of NFPs now prioritise data/reporting — existing tools may not serve this
3. Fragmented systems (multiple disconnected tools) are themselves a coordination barrier
4. Mainstream platforms (Nextdoor, Facebook Groups) optimise for engagement, not community health

### C3. The Tension Remains Unresolved
The research does NOT clearly answer whether communities need a new platform or better support. Both signals are strong:
- **For better support:** The Australian data overwhelmingly shows a strategic/capacity gap, not a tool gap
- **For better tools:** Hylo's unique features (nested groups, cross-group, prosocial design) genuinely don't exist in mainstream options
- **Possible synthesis:** Communities need better support first, AND the coordination tooling landscape has genuine gaps that existing platforms don't fill

## D. Trust as Cross-Cutting Constraint

| Source | Trust Finding |
|--------|--------------|
| Hylo theory of change | Trust must precede complex coordination; trust comes from knowing each other |
| Hylo on tech's role | Tech enhances face-to-face relationships, doesn't replace them |
| Nextdoor failure (I01) | Trust eroded by poor moderation, engagement-optimized algorithms |
| COVID mutual aid (I01) | Trust built through centering people and relationships |
| Australian NFP sector | Digital capability directly affects ability to maintain donor trust |
| Cybersecurity data | 57% lack MFA — trust infrastructure itself is fragile |
| Platform cooperativism (I01) | Community ownership of data/decisions as trust foundation |

**Pattern:** Trust is not a feature to add — it is a precondition that shapes what coordination is even possible. Technology that undermines trust (surveillance, data extraction, engagement optimization) actively harms community coordination regardless of its other capabilities.

## E. Contradictions & Honest Signals

### E1. Contradictions
1. **Hylo says "tech alone is not enough" but is itself a technology project** — honest self-awareness, or strategic positioning?
2. **76% of Australian NFPs use AI but only 1/3 say IT works well** — adoption ≠ effectiveness
3. **Hylo's values are deeply aligned with our Constitution but its reach/sustainability are questionable** — values alignment alone doesn't mean viability
4. **Australian sector wants digital transformation but lacks foundational capacity** — the desire for tools may mask the need for training/support
