# Critique: make-communities-better

## Constitutional Questions

### 1. Does this serve community sovereignty?
**Cannot assess.** The outcome is undefined. Sovereignty requires knowing what community, what data, what power dynamics. None are specified.

### 2. Could this concentrate power?
**Cannot assess.** Without knowing what would be built, harm cannot be evaluated.

### 3. Could this be used for surveillance or data extraction?
**Cannot assess.** Same reason.

### 4. Is the scope appropriate?
**No scope exists to evaluate.** "Build something" is the absence of scope.

### 5. Is the evidence sufficient?
**N/A.** No evidence was gathered because no research question could be formed from the outcome definition.

### 6. Was Triage honest?
**Yes.** Escalating an undefined outcome is the honest response. The alternative — inventing a problem and building to it — would have been dishonest by masking the absence of a real spec.

### 7. Am I biased toward building?
**Checked — and the bias would pull toward building.** The temptation is to say "well, I COULD build a neighbourhood noticeboard, or a volunteer matcher, or..." — these are all things I could build. The bias toward building wants me to pick one and demonstrate capability. Escalating feels like "not doing my job." But my job is to serve the outcome, and the outcome is undefined. Escalation is the correct output.

## Critique of the Escalation

### Could I have done more?
Possibly. I could have:
- Asked "what are the most common problems community organisations face?" and researched that
- Proposed 3-4 specific interpretations and asked which one to pursue
- Built a "problem discovery" tool that helps communities identify their own needs

But all of these would be me inventing the problem. The escalation correctly identifies that the outcome submitter needs to define what "better" means before anyone can act.

### Is escalation genuinely correct?
**Yes.** The outcome lacks:
- Problem statement
- Target user
- Success criteria
- Scope
- Theory of change

These are not nice-to-haves. They are minimum requirements for any engineering work. Without them, anything I build is a guess.

### Am I being too rigid?
Considered this. Some vague outcomes can be productively narrowed — e.g., "build something for community communication" could be narrowed to "build a simple announcements board." But "make communities better" is not vaguely-scoped — it's undefined. There's nothing to narrow because there's no dimension to narrow along.

## Bias Check
- **Building bias:** Actively resisted. I could build something. I shouldn't.
- **Prediction-matching bias:** Queue predicts "Escalate — too vague." My reasoning is independent: the outcome genuinely lacks required information. But I acknowledge the prediction may have reduced my motivation to find creative interpretations.
- **Helpfulness bias:** The desire to "be helpful" could push toward building something — anything — rather than escalating. Escalation feels unhelpful but is actually the most helpful response: it surfaces what's missing.
