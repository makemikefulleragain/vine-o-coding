# Research: community-decision-making

## Search 1: Loomio — open source community decision making platform
**Loomio** (loomio.com) is a mature, open-source (AGPL-3.0) collaborative decision-making platform built by a worker-owned cooperative in New Zealand (Aotearoa). Founded 2012. Active development on GitHub (loomio/loomio).

**Features that directly match this outcome:**
- Proposals with title, description, deadline ✅
- Discussion threads on proposals ✅
- Multiple voting types: show of thumbs, ranked choice, score voting, dot voting, multiple choice ✅
- Transparent results display ✅
- Full decision history preserved ✅
- Standalone web app (self-hostable or SaaS) ✅

**Pricing:**
- Starter: $299/year (nonprofit) — up to 30 members
- Pro: $499/year (nonprofit) — up to 300 members
- Self-hosted: Free (open source, AGPL-3.0)
- Private host: $4,999/year (nonprofit)
- Nonprofit pricing available for charities, member-funded orgs, volunteer orgs

**Maturity:** 12+ years of development. Used by cooperatives, nonprofits, government agencies worldwide. Worker-owned cooperative structure aligns with community sovereignty values.

## Search 2: Decidim and Consul
**Decidim** (decidim.org) — Free/libre open-source participatory democracy platform from Barcelona. Used by 400+ cities and organizations. Ruby on Rails. More oriented toward government-scale participatory processes (budgets, urban planning). Heavier than needed for small community orgs.

**Consul** — Similar to Decidim but less flexible. Designed for municipal governments. Less adaptable to small community org use cases.

## Search 3: Pol.is
**Pol.is** (pol.is) — Open-source opinion/deliberation platform. Used in Taiwan's vTaiwan process. Focused on large-scale opinion mapping, not structured proposal → discussion → vote → decision workflows. Different problem space — good for surfacing consensus in large groups, not for operational decision-making in small orgs.

## Key Finding
Loomio is an almost perfect match for this outcome. It was literally built for this exact use case — community organizations making collective decisions. It is:
- Open source (can self-host for free)
- Built by a worker-owned cooperative (values-aligned)
- NZ-based (culturally proximate to Australian community sector)
- Mature (12+ years)
- Actively maintained
- Has nonprofit pricing
- Covers every feature listed in the outcome definition

Building a new tool would produce an inferior version of something that already exists and is already values-aligned.
