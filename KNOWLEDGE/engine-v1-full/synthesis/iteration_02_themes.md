# Iteration 02 — Synthesis: Themes & Clusters

**Date:** 2025-01-27
**Reframe:** Coordination Support Gap
**Cross-cutting Constraint:** Trust
**Central Tension:** Do communities need a new platform, or better support using existing ones?

---

## Theme Clustering Method

Findings from Iteration 01 (carried forward) and Iteration 02 raw research are clustered under the "Coordination Support Gap" reframe. Each theme is assessed for:
- **Signal strength** (how much evidence supports it)
- **Source convergence** (do independent sources agree)
- **Existing solutions** (what already addresses this)
- **Capacity building lens** (what non-technical support would help)
- **Trust implications** (how does Trust constrain or shape this)

---

## Theme 1: The Capacity Gap Is Bigger Than the Tool Gap

### Evidence
- Only 20% of small-medium Australian NFPs have an IT plan/budget (Liferay/Infoxchange 2025)
- Only 1/3 of Australian NFPs report IT systems work well (Infoxchange 2025)
- 76% use AI tools but foundational infrastructure is missing (Infoxchange 2025)
- "Dangerous gap between aspiration and capability" (Liferay ANZ)
- COVID mutual aid groups succeeded with simple tools + strong human facilitation (Beeck Center, I01)
- Hylo itself says: "successful coordination takes more than technology — it requires deep trust, strong relationships, and a lot of human care" (Hylo About)
- Reddit practitioners: "utilitarian UI" of open-source tools blocks adoption for less-technical members (I01)

### Signal Strength: **Strong**
Multiple independent sources converge on the same finding: the primary barrier to community coordination is not tool availability but strategic capacity, digital literacy, and facilitation skill.

### Existing Solutions
- Infoxchange Digital Transformation Hub (AU) — practical guides, discounts, expert advice
- NTEN (North America) — nonprofit technology capacity building
- Hylo's own facilitation services — "prosocial facilitators that guide member groups on best practices"

### Capacity Building Lens
This theme IS the capacity building lens. The implication is that any KomUnity intervention should prioritise training, documentation, peer support, and community of practice over new software.

### Trust Implications
Capacity gaps erode trust: 57% of NFPs lack MFA, meaning community data is vulnerable. Trust requires competent stewardship of digital tools, not just good intentions.

## Theme 2: Hylo as Existence Proof (Not Destination)

### Evidence
- Hylo's nested groups and cross-group collaboration are genuinely unique, not found in mainstream tools
- Participatory governance (Sociocracy), open-source (Apache 2.0), transparent finances (Open Collective)
- Theory of change explicitly centres trust and relationships before technology
- Bioregional organizing model aligns with place-based community work
- Values deeply aligned with KomUnity Constitution (sovereignty, transparency, community-led)

### But Also
- No evidence of Australian deployment or users
- Small team (5-6 people), donation-funded (~$85K/year) — sustainability risk
- No user testimonials, case studies, or retention data found
- Bay Area-centric community and worldview
- No evidence of non-English language support or accessibility features

### Signal Strength: **Medium**
Hylo proves that a values-aligned, community-led coordination platform CAN exist. But it does not prove it can scale, sustain, or transfer to the Australian context.

### Existing Solutions
Hylo IS an existing solution. The Triage question is: should KomUnity recommend/adopt Hylo, contribute to it, or learn from it?

### Capacity Building Lens
Hylo already bundles facilitation with software ("community leaders and prosocial facilitators"). This is a model worth studying — but the facilitation may matter more than the platform.

### Trust Implications
Hylo's trust architecture is strong: open-source, transparent finances, participatory governance, explicit trust-first theory of change. However, trust also requires proven reliability and sustainability — which are undemonstrated at scale.

## Theme 3: The Australian Voice Gap

### Evidence
- 3 of 8 targeted Australian sources were PDF/binary and inaccessible
- No direct Australian community practitioner voices obtained across 2 iterations
- No Indigenous Australian perspectives on digital coordination accessed
- WACOSS, Volunteering WA, Aboriginal Health Council of WA, Ask Izzy — all referenced but not directly read
- Community Houses Victoria research — referenced but unreadable

### Signal Strength: **Negative signal (gap confirmation)**
This is not evidence FOR a theme — it is evidence that we lack the voices that matter most. Per Constitution: "Real Voices, Not Assumptions."

### Existing Solutions
The voices exist — we just haven't accessed them. This is a research method limitation, not a gap in the world.

### Capacity Building Lens
Any recommendation without Australian community input is premature. Capacity building itself should be co-designed with the communities it serves.

### Trust Implications
Making claims about what Australian communities need without hearing from them would violate the trust principle. This gap must be flagged and addressed before progressing to build decisions.

## Theme 4: Platform Governance as Trust Infrastructure

### Evidence
- Hylo: Sociocracy, open-source, transitioning to participatory stakeholder governance
- Nextdoor failure (I01): volunteer moderators with inconsistent power, engagement-optimized algorithms eroding trust
- Platform cooperativism (I01): community ownership of data/decisions as trust foundation
- Zenzeleni (I01): South African community-owned network as sovereignty model
- Indigenous data sovereignty (I01): data governance frameworks centring community control
- Australian NFPs: cybersecurity vulnerabilities (57% no MFA) undermine governance trust

### Signal Strength: **Strong**
Convergence across academic, practitioner, and platform sources: HOW a platform is governed matters as much or more than WHAT it does. Governance IS trust infrastructure.

### Existing Solutions
- Hylo's governance model (Sociocracy + open-source + transparent finances)
- Loomio (governance decision-making tool)
- Platform cooperativism frameworks
- Indigenous data sovereignty frameworks (CARE Principles)

### Capacity Building Lens
Communities need support to understand and implement governance models, not just access to governed platforms. Governance literacy is itself a capacity-building need.

### Trust Implications
This theme and the trust constraint are nearly identical. Trust-violating governance (surveillance, data extraction, opaque decision-making) should be a hard disqualification for any platform recommendation.

## Synthesis Statement

The strongest signal across two iterations is that **the Coordination Support Gap is primarily a capacity and governance gap, not a technology gap.** Communities struggle not because good tools don't exist, but because they lack the strategic capacity, digital literacy, facilitation skill, and governance frameworks to use ANY tools effectively for coordination.

Hylo demonstrates that a values-aligned platform CAN exist, and its model of bundling facilitation with software is instructive. But recommending or building a new platform without first addressing the capacity gap would repeat the pattern the research itself warns against.

**The central tension update:** The question "new platform vs. better support for existing ones" may be a false binary. The research suggests: **better support first, then evaluate whether existing platforms (including Hylo) meet the remaining coordination needs that capacity alone can't solve.**

**Critical blocker:** No Australian community voices have been directly heard across two iterations. Any specification must acknowledge this and recommend community consultation as a prerequisite to action.
