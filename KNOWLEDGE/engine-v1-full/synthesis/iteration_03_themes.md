# Iteration 03 — Synthesis: Strengths-Based Reframe

**Date:** 2026-02-13
**Previous framing:** "Coordination Support Gap" (I02) — deficit-based
**New framing attempt:** "What do communities already do well, and where does that break down?"
**Cross-cutting constraint:** Trust
**Engine posture:** Handoff preparation — the next useful action is human, not agentic

---

## The Reframe

### From Deficit to Strengths

**I02 framing (deficit):** "Communities face a Coordination Support Gap — they lack strategic capacity, digital literacy, and governance frameworks."

**I03 reframe (strengths-first):** "Australian communities coordinate effectively through physical spaces, trusted relationships, and informal networks. Digital tools become valuable where those existing coordination patterns hit limits — scale, distance, persistence, or cross-group visibility."

### What the Research Actually Shows Communities Do Well

| What Communities Do Well | Evidence Source |
|-------------------------|----------------|
| **Physical gathering spaces** — 1,000+ neighbourhood houses/centres nationally, locally governed, place-based | ANHCA, NHVic, government investment |
| **Informal coordination** — knowledge sharing, referrals, peer support through existing relationships | COVID mutual aid research (I01), Neighbourhood Houses |
| **Adapting tools to context** — BSS mutual aid served 1,400 households with Google Sheets + phone hotlines + paper flyers | Beeck Center (I01) |
| **Centering people over tech** — phone hotlines used MORE than online forms by people needing help | BSS case study (I01) |
| **Trust-first organising** — effective groups build relationships before introducing complex coordination | Hylo theory of change, mutual aid research |
| **Resource sharing networks** — WACOSS CORE enables sharing between business and NFP sectors | WACOSS (I03) |
| **Cloud adoption** — 87% of NFPs now use cloud collaboration tools (up from 24% a decade ago) | Infoxchange 2025 |
| **AI curiosity** — 67% of orgs exploring generative AI, mostly for content creation | Infoxchange 2025 |

### Where It Breaks Down

| Where Coordination Breaks Down | Evidence Source |
|-------------------------------|----------------|
| **When the tech person leaves** — systems collapse because knowledge isn't shared | Reddit practitioners (I01), sector reports |
| **At scale** — informal coordination works for small groups but doesn't scale across groups or regions | Hylo's nested groups rationale (I02) |
| **At the digital-physical boundary** — physical spaces coordinate well, but their digital presence is often fragmented | Infoxchange data: only 1/3 say IT works well |
| **When security matters** — 20% of volunteer-led orgs use MFA; 77% have no cybersecurity plan | Infoxchange 2025 (I03) |
| **When evidence is needed** — only 22% can measure service impact; funders increasingly demand data | Infoxchange 2025 via Liferay (I02) |
| **When resources compete with mission** — "difficult decision to decide how much to invest in IT vs direct services" | David Crosbie, CCA (I03) |
| **When barriers are ethical, not technical** — data security, ethics, sovereignty, privacy are top barriers to AI adoption, not cost | Infoxchange 2025 (I03) |

### The Reframed Question

Not: "What's the gap?" but: **"What existing community coordination strengths could be amplified, and what breakdowns could be prevented, with the right support?"**

This is the question the community listening package should explore.

### What the Ecosystem Doesn't Yet Cover

| Gap | Notes |
|-----|-------|
| **Community listening at scale** | Existing relationships provide access; structured conversation framework is needed (this iteration's output) |
| **Strengths documentation** | No current way to capture what communities already do well before proposing changes |
| **Cross-group coordination** | The campaign model (individual needs → quests → vision) conceptually addresses this but hasn't been tested with real community groups |
| **Indigenous engagement pathway** | AIATSIS Code of Ethics requirements not yet integrated into any engagement process |
| **Governance literacy for communities** | Neither NP Digital Solutions nor CoachFlux explicitly covers helping communities evaluate platform governance (data ownership, decision-making, etc.) |

## Theme Ranking (Iteration 03)

### Theme 1: The Handoff Is the Highest-Value Action

**Signal:** The engine has reached the point described in ENGINE.md §Recognising the Handoff Point. Three iterations of web research have converged on the same finding: the next useful action requires human engagement, not more research. The existing community relationships (ALIKE WA, The Pack Music, WALGA) provide the access. What's needed is a structured conversation framework and supporting materials.

**Actionability:** High — this IS the spec for this iteration.

### Theme 2: Communities Coordinate Well Already — The Question Is Where It Breaks

**Signal:** Strong convergence across all three iterations. The strengths-based reframe is grounded in evidence, not imposed. Neighbourhood houses, mutual aid networks, and informal coordination are real and effective. Breakdowns are specific and documentable.

**Actionability:** This reframe should shape the conversation guide — asking about strengths first, breakdowns second.

### Theme 3: Ethical Engagement Requires Different Approaches for Different Communities

**Signal:** AIATSIS Code of Ethics makes clear that Aboriginal community engagement cannot follow the same process as general community listening. This is a boundary, not a gap.

**Actionability:** The listening package must distinguish between communities where Mike has existing relationships (ALIKE WA, The Pack Music, WALGA) and communities where engagement requires different protocols (Aboriginal community-controlled organisations).

### Theme 4: NP Digital Solutions and CoachFlux Already Cover Some Capacity Building

**Signal:** The Constitution documents these components. Any capacity-building recommendation from previous iterations must be checked against them.

**Actionability:** The conversation guide should explore what NP Digital Solutions and CoachFlux already do, and where community needs exceed their current coverage — informing whether to extend existing components or identify genuine gaps.

## Synthesis Statement

**Three iterations of research converge on one clear action: prepare for human community engagement, then hand off.**

The engine cannot substitute for the conversations that need to happen. It can ensure those conversations are well-prepared, grounded in research, sensitive to ethical boundaries, and connected to the existing Kamunity ecosystem.

The strengths-based reframe is: **Communities already coordinate well. The question for listening is: where does that coordination hit limits, and what kind of support (not necessarily technology) would help?**

The existing ecosystem means this isn't starting from scratch — it's extending relationships and components that already exist.
