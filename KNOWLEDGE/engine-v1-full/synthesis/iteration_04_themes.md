## The Model Update Test

I01-03 converged on: "The Coordination Support Gap is primarily a capacity and governance gap, not a technology gap. Capacity first, then tools."

Iteration 04's structured web listening tested this synthesis against actual WA/Australian community web discourse. Here is an honest assessment of what held up, what was challenged, and what was new.

---

## What Held Up (Confirmed)

### Finding 1: Capacity gap > tool gap — CONFIRMED, with nuance

**Evidence:** Pack Music's crowdfunding priorities list "artist onboarding & community outreach: workshops, Living Labs coordination, and support staff" alongside tech development — the human coordination work is equally or more important than the platform. WALGA is investing $900K in cybersecurity training and capacity building, not in buying new tools. Volunteering WA has built 350+ resources, a national knowledge base, workshops, and national standards — the infrastructure IS capacity building.

**Nuance added:** The capacity gap is confirmed at the USE level (communities using digital tools) but the web listening revealed a capacity gap at the BUILD level too — Pack Music lost their original developers when the company went into administration. Community-owned platforms face a specific kind of fragility: they depend on technical contributors who may leave, and they can't pay market rates to replace them.

### Finding 2: Communities coordinate well already — CONFIRMED, strongly

**Evidence:** ALIKE WA has been coordinating peer support across WA for 40+ years, now serving 780+ groups. Volunteering WA leads national strategy implementation. Pack Music coordinates across multiple Perth LGAs, artists, businesses, and fans through a co-operative structure. These are not struggling organisations waiting for tools — they are effective coordinators with established methods.

### Finding 3: Trust as precondition — CONFIRMED, with new evidence type

**Evidence:** Pack Music's co-operative structure is itself a trust architecture — subscriber fees go directly to artists, transparent and fair. ALIKE WA's values (Accountability, Empowerment, Belonging, Humanity, Collaboration) are trust-building values. Kamunity.org's tagline "no algorithms, no data mining" explicitly positions against trust-violating platform patterns. Pack Music choosing Mastodon and Bluesky over mainstream platforms demonstrates values-driven platform selection in practice.

**New evidence type:** These aren't statements ABOUT trust — they're structural choices that EMBODY trust. The co-operative model, the ethical platform choices, the transparent financial flows. Trust isn't just talked about; it's built into governance, architecture, and daily decisions.

## What Was Challenged

### Challenge 1: "Communities don't need new tools" — PARTIALLY CHALLENGED

**Previous synthesis:** I02 concluded "capacity first, then evaluate tools" with an implication that existing tools might be adequate with better support.

**Challenge from Pack Music:** The Pack IS building a new tool because Spotify is structurally hostile to their values and their musicians' livelihoods. Their prorated payment model concentrates money on major-label artists. No amount of capacity building on Spotify will make it serve local independent musicians. The tool itself is the problem, and building a new one is the correct response.

**Assessment:** The "capacity first" model holds for communities whose coordination challenges are about USING tools (small NFPs, volunteer groups, neighbourhood houses). But it breaks down for communities whose challenge is that **existing platforms are extractive by design.** For those communities, building IS the right answer — and it's not a Triage failure. It's Triage working correctly: they evaluated existing solutions (Spotify), found them structurally hostile, and are building.

**Model update:** The I01-03 synthesis should be amended: "Capacity first, then tools" applies when existing tools are neutral or adequate. When existing platforms are extractive by design, **build is not a failure of Triage — it's the correct Triage outcome at Step 5.**

### Challenge 2: "Start with capacity, then evaluate tools" may not reflect all community contexts

**Previous synthesis:** I03 recommended listening first, then evaluating whether tool needs exist.

**Challenge from ALIKE WA:** They explicitly describe themselves as "embracing new technologies" and "staying ahead of the curve." This isn't a capacity-deficient organisation waiting to be supported. They appear ready for, and possibly already evaluating, new tools. The Pack Music already knows exactly what they need and are building it.

**Assessment:** The "listen first" recommendation from I03 remains correct as a general approach for communities we haven't heard from yet. But for communities that have ALREADY articulated their needs publicly (Pack Music, ALIKE WA), the listening has partially been done — their web presence IS their expressed voice.

**Model update:** The listening package from I03 should be adapted per relationship: for The Pack Music, the conversation is about what Kamunity's ecosystem can offer their specific challenges, not about discovering what they need. For ALIKE WA, the question is what 780 peer support groups need, which the peak body may or may not fully represent.

## What Was New (Not in I01-03)

### New Finding 1: Developer Dependency Risk for Community-Owned Platforms

Pack Music lost their original developers when the company went into administration. This is a specific version of the "tech person leaves" pattern identified in I01, but at a higher level — it's not a volunteer leaving a community group, it's a development company failing during a commissioned build.

**Implication for Kamunity:** FactoryK's constitutional safeguards and autonomous build pipeline could specifically address this — providing community orgs with development capacity that isn't dependent on a single vendor. This is a concrete use case for the existing ecosystem.

### New Finding 2: Living Labs as a Coordination Model

Pack Music's "Living Labs" concept embeds local music in public spaces and businesses through local government partnerships (Fremantle, Vincent, Subiaco). This is place-based coordination — not a digital tool but a method of connecting community assets (local music) with community spaces (businesses, libraries) through institutional partnerships (LGAs).

**Implication:** This aligns with bioregional organising (from Hylo research, I02) but is LOCAL and PRACTICAL. It's a coordination model that could apply to other community domains. Worth exploring whether Kamunity's campaign model (individual needs → quests → community vision) maps to this.

### New Finding 3: INVOLVE Already Exists

Volunteering WA has a volunteer management platform called INVOLVE with Premium, Essentials, and Free tiers. This is a WA-based, sector-specific tool built by the peak body for volunteering.

**Triage implication:** Before any recommendation involving volunteer coordination, INVOLVE must be evaluated. It may already cover the need. This is Triage Step 1 (Find) in action.

### New Finding 4: Barriers to Volunteering Research Covers Missing Voices

Volunteering WA commissioned research in 2023 on barriers to volunteering for First Nations people, people with disability, and newly arrived migrants. These are precisely the voices most missing from our web listening AND most challenging to reach through digital methods.

**Implication:** This research partially addresses the "missing voices" gap from I01-03. It should be accessed and integrated — these are Tier 1 sources (research with direct community participation, commissioned by peak body).

### New Finding 5: Ethical Platform Choices as Daily Sovereignty Practice

Pack Music's presence on Mastodon and Bluesky (instead of or alongside mainstream platforms) demonstrates that sovereignty isn't just about data ownership — it's about daily choices of where you show up online. This is the sovereignty calculator concept from the Constitution made real.

## Synthesis Statement

**The I01-03 synthesis holds but needs one key amendment.**

"Capacity first, then tools" is correct for communities whose coordination challenges are about using existing tools better. But it's incomplete. Some communities face a different challenge: **existing platforms are structurally hostile to their values.** For them, building is not a Triage failure — it's the correct Triage outcome.

The amended synthesis: **"Capacity first for communities learning to coordinate digitally. Build when existing platforms are extractive by design. And in both cases, trust architecture (governance, transparency, ownership) is the foundation."**

The web listening also revealed something the previous iterations missed by treating Kamunity as greenfield: **the ecosystem's existing relationships are themselves a rich research data source.** Pack Music's public discourse revealed developer dependency risk, Living Labs coordination, and values-driven platform choices. ALIKE WA's public presence revealed the 780-group scale challenge. These are specific, actionable signals that generic community sector research didn't surface.

The Community Listening Package from I03 remains the right next human action — but it should be informed by what the web listening revealed about each specific relationship.
