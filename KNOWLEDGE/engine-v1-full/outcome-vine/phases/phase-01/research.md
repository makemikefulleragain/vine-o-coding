# Phase 1 Research — Make It Clear

**Date:** 2026-02-15
**Searches used:** 5/5

---

## Research Question

What does a landing page need to explain Outcome Vine Coding in 60 seconds to someone with no coding experience?

## Key Findings

### 1. What Makes Teaching Sites Work for Beginners

- **Hero clarity is everything.** Education landing pages that convert share one trait: the hero section answers "what is this and why should I care?" in under 10 seconds (Teachable, Lapa Ninja education category analysis).
- **Z-pattern layout** works for simple landing pages — eye moves top-left → top-right → bottom-left → bottom-right. Put the value proposition top-left, CTA along the Z path (Ramotion, 99designs, Unbounce).
- **Three-step process sections** are the dominant pattern for explaining methodologies. freeCodeCamp, Codecademy, and similar sites reduce complex processes to 3 visual steps before offering depth.
- **Show, don't tell** — the best teaching sites (freeCodeCamp, Codecademy) put interactive elements front and center. Static explanation alone reads as a blog post.

### 2. The Competitive Landscape for "Build an App with AI"

- **No methodology sites exist.** The AI coding space is dominated by tool comparison guides (Builder.io, Droids on Roids) and prompt engineering tutorials. Nobody is teaching a structured *process* for AI-assisted development.
- **Replit** is the closest analog — "build apps with AI" positioning, accessible to non-programmers. But it's a platform, not a methodology. Users are locked in.
- **The gap is real:** searches for "how to build an app with AI" return tool lists, not process guides. Outcome Vine Coding fills a genuine void.

### 3. What Causes Beginners to Bounce

- **Jargon in the first 3 seconds.** If Maya sees "CI/CD pipeline" or "agile sprint" before she understands what the site does, she's gone.
- **Wall of text.** Education landing pages with paragraphs above the fold have significantly higher bounce rates than those with visual hierarchy (Unbounce research).
- **No proof.** Claims without evidence ("build anything!") trigger skepticism. A link to a real, live tool built with the process is powerful social proof.
- **No clear next step.** If the CTA is vague ("Learn More"), users don't know what they're committing to.

### 4. Visual Design Patterns That Work

- **Split hero:** Left side = headline + subheadline + CTA. Right side = visual/illustration. Clean, modern, high contrast.
- **Process steps as horizontal cards** with icons — 3 columns on desktop, stacked on mobile.
- **Proof section:** Screenshot or link to the real product, with a brief story ("We built this in one session").
- **Color:** Indigo/blue family conveys trust and clarity. The grants-hub uses indigo — visual continuity makes sense.

### 5. What Maya Needs vs What Brian Needs (on a Landing Page)

| Need | Maya | Brian |
|---|---|---|
| First impression | "I can do this" | "This is rigorous" |
| Proof | "Someone built a real thing" | "The methodology has theoretical grounding" |
| CTA | "Try the widget" (Phase 2) | "Read the case study" (Phase 3) |
| Tone | Warm, direct, no jargon | Professional but accessible |

**For Phase 1:** Maya's needs come first. Brian's depth comes in Phases 2-3. The landing page should feel approachable, not academic.

---

## Implications for Phase 1

1. **Hero section** must answer: "What is this?" + "Who is it for?" + "Does it work?" in ~30 words.
2. **Three-layer visual** (Foundation Docs → Phase Loop → Safety Mechanisms) as the core explanation — but with Maya-friendly labels, not methodology jargon.
3. **Proof section** with live link to grants-hub.netlify.app and a 2-sentence story.
4. **CTA** should point to the widget (which doesn't exist yet) — use a "Coming soon" or "Get started" placeholder that becomes active in Phase 2.
5. **Mobile-first** — Maya is likely on her phone.
6. **No Brian lean-in sections on the landing page** — keep it clean. Brian's depth is Phase 3.
7. **Indigo/blue color family** for visual continuity with grants-hub.

## Failure Modes to Watch

- Over-explaining the methodology on the landing page (save depth for Phase 3)
- Making the three layers sound technical instead of intuitive
- Forgetting mobile responsiveness
- Building a beautiful page that doesn't actually explain anything clearly
