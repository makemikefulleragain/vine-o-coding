## 1. Seven Constitutional Questions

### Q1: Does this serve community sovereignty, or does it create dependency?

**Answer: Serves sovereignty.**

The listening package explicitly asks communities what THEY do well and what THEY need. It doesn't propose solutions. The one-pager states "we're not selling a product" and "we're not here to tell you what you need." The conversation guide leads with strengths, not deficits. The AIATSIS boundary honours Indigenous self-determination.

No dependency is created — communities share what they choose, receive a summary back, and decide whether to engage further.

**Verdict: PASS**

### Q2: Am I building because it's needed, or because building is what I do?

**Answer: Not building at all.**

Triage resolved at Connect + Extend. The deliverables are conversation documents, not software. This is the engine fulfilling its purpose per ENGINE.md §Handoff Point: "getting to the point where human action is the clear next step, with the best possible preparation."

**Verdict: PASS**

### Q3: Does something already exist that does this well enough?

**Answer: Partially.**

Community consultation frameworks exist (IAP2 spectrum, participatory action research, human-centred design interviews). The specific value of this package is:
- Customised to Kamunity's context, existing relationships, and research findings
- Strengths-based framing grounded in 3 iterations of sector research
- Awareness of the existing ecosystem (so findings can be mapped to NP Digital Solutions, CoachFlux, etc.)
- AIATSIS boundary explicitly built in

A generic consultation framework would work but wouldn't carry the accumulated research context or ecosystem awareness.

**Verdict: PASS — extends existing methods with specific context**

### Q4: Could this be misused to concentrate power or extract value?

**Answer: Low risk, one caveat.**

The listening conversations are designed to be reciprocal (share back what you heard). They don't collect personal data. They ask for permission to take notes. The one-pager is honest about what Kamunity is and isn't doing.

**Caveat:** If the findings from conversations are used to build something WITHOUT returning to the communities who contributed, that would be extractive. The conversation guide includes "share a summary back" as a commitment — this must be honoured.

**Verdict: PASS with commitment to follow-through**

### Q5: Am I optimising for the measurable at the expense of the illegible?

**Answer: The package explicitly centres the illegible.**

"What to listen for" sections focus on trust, relationships, informal infrastructure, who the connectors are, and what "coordination" means to the person speaking. These are illegible by definition. The package does not include satisfaction scales, net promoter scores, or quantitative frameworks.

**Verdict: PASS**

### Q6: Would a real community organisation actually use this? Why or why not?

**Answer: The documents are for Mike to use WITH community organisations, not for them to use.**

The conversation guide is designed for a real person having a real conversation. The one-pager is plain language, no jargon, honest about what Kamunity is. The target list starts with people Mike already knows.

**Risk:** The conversation guide is long. In practice, Mike will need to internalise the structure and use it flexibly, not read questions from a script. The "what to listen for" sections are coaching notes, not a checklist.

**Verdict: PASS — but usability depends on Mike adapting it to his style**

### Q7: Is the need I identified real, or did I construct it from cherry-picked sources?

**Answer: The need (community listening) was identified by the human, not constructed by the engine.**

Mike's guidance explicitly stated: "The 'listen first' recommendation is constitutionally correct, and the engine cannot conduct those conversations. Iteration 03's highest-value output is preparing materials for human community engagement." The engine is fulfilling a directive, not inventing a need.

The underlying research finding (communities coordinate well already; breakdowns are specific; support needs are unclear without asking) is grounded in 3 iterations of evidence from 10+ independent sources.

**Verdict: PASS**

## 4. Bias Identification

### Bias 1: Conversation Guide May Inadvertently Lead
The strengths-first structure is good practice, but "what to listen for" sections could unconsciously bias Mike toward hearing what the research predicts. Mitigation: Mike should stay open to surprises and note when findings DON'T match the research.

**Severity: Low.** The guide explicitly includes "Surprise" as a tagging category.

### Bias 2: Target List Reflects Existing Network
Starting with existing relationships is constitutionally correct but creates a selection bias — Mike will hear from people who already know him, who may share his values and perspectives. Communities with no connection to ALIKE WA, The Pack Music, or WALGA won't be heard.

**Severity: Medium.** Partially mitigated by snowball sampling (asking "who else should I talk to?"). But genuinely disconnected communities — the digitally excluded, the geographically remote — won't be reached through this method.

### Bias 3: English-Language and Digitally-Accessible Framing
The entire package assumes conversations happen in English with people who can be reached through standard communication channels. Multicultural community groups, refugee communities, and communities with limited English proficiency are not addressed.

**Severity: Medium.** This is a real limitation. The package should acknowledge it, even if it can't solve it in this phase.

### Bias 4: WA-Centric by Design
The Constitution says Perth/WA is primary context. This is correct — but it means findings won't generalise to other Australian states without separate research. This is acceptable for now but should be noted.

**Severity: Low.** By design, not by accident.

## 5. What the Package Gets Right

1. **Handoff is correctly identified.** The engine has done what it can do (research, synthesis, preparation). The next action is human.
2. **Strengths-based reframe is grounded.** Not imposed — it comes from the evidence (neighbourhood houses, mutual aid, cloud adoption, AI curiosity).
3. **Existing ecosystem is woven through.** Not a separate section to ignore — it shapes the target list, the "after conversation" notes, and the context brief.
4. **AIATSIS boundary is explicit.** Not a footnote — it's a dedicated section with clear reasoning and alternative pathway.
5. **Triage resolves before Build.** Connect + Extend. No software needed.

## 6. What Could Be Improved

1. **The conversation guide is long.** Mike may benefit from a laminated "cheat sheet" version — 5 key questions on one card.
2. **No guidance on how to synthesise across conversations.** After 5-8 conversations, how does Mike cluster findings? A simple tagging/synthesis template would help.
3. **No timeline or commitment structure.** "4-6 weeks" is mentioned but there's no check-in point or accountability mechanism.
4. **Multicultural community access not addressed.** Should at minimum be flagged as a known gap.
