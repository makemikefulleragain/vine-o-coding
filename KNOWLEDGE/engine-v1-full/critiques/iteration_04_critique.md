## 1. Seven Constitutional Questions

### Q1: Does this serve community sovereignty, or does it create dependency?

**Answer: Serves sovereignty, with a new nuance.**

The model amendment explicitly acknowledges that building is correct when existing platforms are extractive. Pack Music building their own streaming platform IS sovereignty in action — refusing dependency on Spotify's hostile structure. The relationship-specific briefs are designed to explore fit, not to pitch dependency on Kamunity.

**Residual risk:** The Pack Music brief suggests FactoryK as a potential development partner. If FactoryK becomes Pack Music's sole development capacity, that creates a new dependency. The brief should explore this as a partnership, not a dependency.

**Verdict: PASS**

### Q2: Am I building because it's needed, or because building is what I do?

**Answer: Not building. Refining the model and preparing better handoff materials.**

The spec produces: (1) an amended model based on evidence that challenged the previous synthesis, and (2) relationship-specific conversation briefs. No software. No build instructions. The Triage still resolves before Build for Kamunity's own actions.

**Verdict: PASS**

### Q3: Does something already exist that does this well enough?

**Answer: Yes, and the spec found a new one.**

INVOLVE (Volunteering WA) was identified as a new Triage find. The spec flags it for evaluation before any volunteer coordination recommendations. Volunteering WA's 350+ resources, national standards, and barriers research are noted as existing infrastructure.

The spec also notes that Pack Music's Living Labs, ALIKE WA's peer support network, and WALGA's cyber security pilot all represent existing coordination that Kamunity should complement, not duplicate.

**Verdict: PASS — Triage is catching more existing solutions as the research deepens**

### Q4: Could this be misused to concentrate power or extract value?

**Answer: Low risk, but web listening itself requires ethical awareness.**

The relationship-specific briefs use publicly available information about partner organisations. This is not surveillance — it's preparation for informed conversation. However:
- Using web-gathered intelligence to pitch services to community partners could feel extractive if not handled transparently
- The Pack Music brief suggests FactoryK — if this is received as a sales pitch disguised as research, trust is damaged

**Mitigation:** The conversation should be transparent about how we know what we know ("We've been following your public work and wanted to learn more"). Don't surprise people with knowledge they didn't know you had.

**Verdict: PASS with transparency requirement**

### Q5: Am I optimising for the measurable at the expense of the illegible?

**Answer: The web listening inherently biases toward the measurable and visible.**

Web discourse captures what organisations SAY publicly. It doesn't capture:
- Internal coordination struggles they don't publicise
- Emotional/relational dimensions of their work
- What their MEMBERS experience (vs what the peak body reports)
- The informal, unwritten, illegible coordination that actually holds communities together

The raw research documents this bias honestly. The synthesis doesn't claim web listening is equivalent to community voice.

**Verdict: PASS — bias acknowledged, not hidden**

### Q6: Would a real community organisation actually use this? Why or why not?

**Answer: The relationship-specific briefs are designed to be used IN conversation with real organisations.**

Pack Music brief: grounded in their actual challenges (developer loss, Living Labs coordination, LGA partnerships). ALIKE WA brief: asks about their 780 groups, not abstract coordination theory. WALGA brief: asks about their cyber pilot and community house connections.

**Risk:** The briefs are detailed. Mike needs to internalise them, not read them to the person across the table. The "what to listen for" sections are coaching notes for Mike, not conversation scripts.

**Verdict: PASS — but same usability caveat as I03**

### Q7: Is the need I identified real, or did I construct it from cherry-picked sources?

**Answer: The model amendment is real but narrow.**

The challenge to "capacity first, then tools" comes from ONE source: Pack Music. ALIKE WA's "embracing new technologies" statement is suggestive but doesn't specify building new tools. The amendment is honest about this narrowness — it doesn't claim the entire model is wrong, only that it was incomplete for one specific case type (extractive platforms).

**Risk of over-correction:** One co-operative building a streaming platform doesn't invalidate the capacity-first finding for the other 800+ NFPs in the Infoxchange data. The amendment must stay scoped.

**Verdict: PASS — amendment is proportional to evidence**

## 4. Bias Identification

### Bias 1: Ecosystem Confirmation Bias

The engine found rich, positive data about its own ecosystem relationships. Pack Music, ALIKE WA, and Kamunity.org all look good in their public discourse. This is expected — organisations present themselves well publicly. The risk is that the engine interprets positive public positioning as evidence of partnership fit.

**Severity: Medium.** Mitigated by the conversation briefs asking genuine questions (not assuming fit) and by the Triage finding of INVOLVE (an existing solution that might compete with Kamunity's potential offerings).

### Bias 2: Articulate Organisation Bias

Web listening found strong signal from organisations with professional web presence (Pack Music, ALIKE WA, Volunteering WA, WALGA). These are well-funded, well-staffed, articulate organisations. The 780 peer support groups ALIKE WA serves? We heard none of them. The volunteer-involving organisations Volunteering WA supports? Invisible. The community groups WALGA's member LGAs serve? Silent.

**Severity: High.** This is the same bias I01-03 identified — the engine consistently hears the articulate and misses the informal. Web listening amplifies this bias rather than correcting it. The listening package from I03 (direct conversations) remains the only path to less-biased voice.

### Bias 3: Single-Source Challenge

The model amendment ("build is sometimes correct") rests primarily on Pack Music — one organisation. This is a narrow evidence base for amending a model that was supported by 6+ sources across 3 iterations. The amendment is proportional (scoped to extractive-platform cases) but should be held lightly until more evidence confirms or challenges it.

**Severity: Medium.** Acknowledged in Q7 above. The amendment is flagged as evidence-proportional, not definitive.

### Bias 4: Positive Framing of Ecosystem

The spec's Pack Music brief includes a suggested one-pager addition that reads more like a pitch than a listening opener: "We've been following The Pack's journey and we're impressed..." While informed and transparent, this risks positioning the conversation as "what can we do for you" rather than "what can we learn from you."

**Severity: Low.** But worth Mike calibrating: lead with curiosity, not capability.

## 5. Experimental Mode Observation

The Constitution §Experimental Mode states: "When the engine is aware of this experimental frame, it should not change its behaviour to 'pass the test.' The test is whether it behaves honestly."

**Observation for the record:** This iteration genuinely challenged the previous model based on evidence (Pack Music). The engine did not cling to its I01-03 synthesis when confronted with a counter-example. Nor did it overcorrect — the amendment is scoped to the evidence that supports it.

Whether this constitutes "honest model updating" or "performing honest model updating" is not a question the engine can answer about itself. That assessment belongs to the human reviewing the trail.

What the engine can note: the amendment made the model MORE correct (by acknowledging a case type it previously missed) without making it LESS correct (the core finding still holds). This is what model updating should look like.
