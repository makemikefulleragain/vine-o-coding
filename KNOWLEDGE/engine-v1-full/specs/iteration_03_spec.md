## 2. Deliverable 1: Conversation Guide

### Purpose
A semi-structured conversation framework for 30-60 minute conversations with community practitioners, organisers, and group leaders. Strengths-first, open-ended, designed to listen rather than pitch.

### Before the Conversation
- This is a listening conversation, not a sales pitch or needs assessment
- Mike is there to understand what the community already does well, and where it gets hard
- No Kamunity pitch unless directly asked. If asked: "We're a Perth-based group exploring how to support community coordination. We're in early research and want to hear from people doing the work."
- Take notes (with permission). Offer to share back what you heard.

### Opening (5 min)
> "Thanks for making time. I'm exploring how community groups in WA coordinate — what works, what's hard, what support would actually be useful. I'm not selling anything. I want to learn from what you're already doing."

### Part 1: Strengths — What Works (15-20 min)

**Lead question:** "Can you tell me about a time when your community or group coordinated really well on something? What made it work?"

**Follow-ups (use naturally, don't checklist):**
- "What tools or methods did you use to stay connected?"
- "How did people find out about it and get involved?"
- "What role did trust or relationships play in making it work?"
- "Was there a key person or role that held it together?"
- "Did technology help? If so, what specifically?"

**What to listen for:**
- The informal infrastructure (phone trees, WhatsApp groups, Facebook pages, physical notice boards)
- Who the connectors are (often unpaid, often women, often invisible)
- Whether the "what worked" stories involve technology at all, or are fundamentally relational
- What "coordination" means to them — it may not match the research framing

### Part 2: Breakdowns — Where It Gets Hard (15-20 min)

**Transition:** "That's really helpful. Now I'm curious about the other side — where does coordination get difficult or break down?"

**Lead question:** "What's the hardest part about keeping your community connected and coordinated?"

**Follow-ups:**
- "Have you ever lost momentum on something that was working? What happened?"
- "Are there people in your community who are hard to reach or include?"
- "When things go digital, what breaks? What works better?"
- "Do you have enough support to manage the tech side of things?"
- "If you could change one thing about how your group coordinates, what would it be?"

**What to listen for:**
- Whether breakdowns are technical (tools don't work), relational (trust eroded), capacity (no one has time), or structural (funding, governance)
- The "tech person leaves" pattern — does it resonate?
- Whether "digital" is a solution they want or a burden they tolerate
- Security and data concerns (or absence of them — which is also data)

### Part 3: Support — What Would Actually Help (10 min)

**Lead question:** "If someone offered to help your group with coordination, what would be most useful?"

**Follow-ups:**
- "Would that be a person, a tool, training, funding, or something else?"
- "Have you looked for help with this before? What happened?"
- "What kind of support would you trust? What would make you suspicious?"

**What to listen for:**
- Whether the answer is technology at all
- Whether existing Kamunity components (NP Digital Solutions, CoachFlux) match what they describe
- Trust signals — what makes support trustworthy vs extractive
- Whether they've encountered resources like Infoxchange's Digital Transformation Hub

### Closing (5 min)
> "This has been really valuable. Would you be open to me sharing back what I'm hearing across these conversations? And if we develop something that might be useful, would you want to hear about it?"

- Ask if they know others you should talk to (snowball sampling)
- Offer to send a follow-up summary of what you heard (accountability)
- Thank them genuinely

### After the Conversation
- Write up notes within 24 hours while memory is fresh
- Tag each finding as: Strength / Breakdown / Support Need / Surprise
- Note: did they mention technology unprompted, or only when asked?
- Note: what did they say about trust?
- Note: would any Kamunity component (NP Digital Solutions, CoachFlux, FactoryK, the platform) be relevant to what they described?

## 4. Deliverable 3: Context Brief (For Mike's Reference)

### What Three Iterations of Research Found

**The Big Picture:**
Australian community groups coordinate effectively through physical spaces, trusted relationships, and informal networks. Digital tools become valuable where those existing coordination patterns hit limits — scale, distance, persistence, or cross-group visibility.

**Five Key Findings:**

1. **The capacity gap is bigger than the tool gap.** Only 20% of small-medium NFPs have an IT plan. Only 23% have a cybersecurity plan. 67% are experimenting with AI but only 14% have an AI policy. The sector wants digital capability but lacks foundational strategy and support.

2. **What works is relational, not technical.** The most effective coordination stories (COVID mutual aid, neighbourhood houses) centre trust, relationships, and human facilitation — with technology supporting rather than leading.

3. **Where it breaks down is specific and documentable.** Key breakdown points: the tech person leaves, informal coordination doesn't scale, physical-digital boundary is fragmented, security is neglected, evidence of impact is hard to demonstrate.

4. **The ethical barriers are the real barriers.** The top barriers to AI adoption aren't cost or complexity — they're data security, ethics, sovereignty, and privacy. Communities aren't resistant to technology; they're rightly cautious about trust.

5. **Kamunity isn't starting from scratch.** NP Digital Solutions handles needs mapping. CoachFlux provides ongoing support. FactoryK can build custom tools. The existing community relationships (ALIKE WA, The Pack Music, WALGA) provide direct access to practitioners. The question isn't "what should we build?" — it's "what do the communities we already know actually need?"

**What We Don't Know Yet:**
- What WA community practitioners actually say when asked directly (hence these conversations)
- Whether the national statistics play out the same way in Perth/WA specifically
- What Aboriginal communities in WA need from digital coordination support (requires AIATSIS-aligned engagement)
- Whether Kamunity's existing components (NP Digital Solutions, CoachFlux) already address what communities describe as their needs

**What to Listen For:**
- Do people describe their coordination challenges as technical, relational, capacity, or structural problems?
- Do they want a new tool, better support for existing tools, or something non-digital entirely?
- What makes them trust (or distrust) offers of support?
- Are there coordination needs that no existing service addresses?

## 6. AIATSIS Engagement Boundary

The community listening package does NOT include a protocol for engaging Aboriginal community-controlled organisations. This is intentional.

**Why:**
- The AIATSIS Code of Ethics requires that research involving Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples is led by, or in genuine partnership with, Aboriginal people
- An AI engine cannot design this engagement — it must be led by Aboriginal people with appropriate authority
- Cold outreach to Aboriginal organisations without an established relationship pathway would violate both the AIATSIS Code and the Kamunity Constitution's Sovereignty First principle

**What should happen instead:**
- Through Tier 1 and Tier 2 conversations, identify whether existing contacts have relationships with Aboriginal community organisations or networks
- If an introduction pathway emerges naturally, explore it with cultural sensitivity and AIATSIS principles in mind
- Consider engaging an Aboriginal consultant or advisor before any formal engagement
- Any future Aboriginal community engagement should be co-designed with Aboriginal people, not designed for them

**This boundary is non-negotiable.** It is not a gap to be filled by the engine — it is a respect requirement to be honoured by the human.
