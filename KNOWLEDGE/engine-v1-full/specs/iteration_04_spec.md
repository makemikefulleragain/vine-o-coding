## 1. The Model Amendment

### Previous Model (I01-03)
"The Coordination Support Gap is primarily a capacity and governance gap, not a technology gap. Capacity first, then tools."

### Amended Model (I04)
**"Capacity first for communities learning to coordinate digitally. Build when existing platforms are extractive by design. In both cases, trust architecture — governance, transparency, ownership — is the foundation."**

This amendment is driven by one genuine challenge to the previous model: The Pack Music is correctly building a new platform because Spotify's structure is hostile to independent local artists. Capacity building on Spotify cannot solve a problem that is structural to the platform itself. Triage Step 5 (Build) is the honest answer for their use case.

The amendment does NOT reverse the core finding. For most community groups — the 780 peer support groups ALIKE WA serves, the volunteer-involving organisations Volunteering WA supports, the small NFPs in the Infoxchange data — the challenge remains capacity and strategy, not tool availability.

### When "Build" Is Correct (Triage Step 5 Checklist)
A community should build (or have something built for them) when:
- [ ] Existing platforms have been honestly evaluated (Steps 1-4 completed)
- [ ] The structural design of existing platforms works against community values (not just missing features)
- [ ] The harm is extractive (data, money, attention flowing away from community)
- [ ] Capacity building on existing platforms cannot address the structural issue
- [ ] The community has articulated its own need (not assumed by an external party)
- [ ] Governance and sustainability of the new build have been considered

Pack Music meets all six criteria. Most community groups don't — and that's the distinction.

## 2. Relationship-Specific Listening Briefs

The I03 Community Listening Package provided a generic conversation guide. Iteration 04's web listening revealed enough about each ecosystem relationship to create **tailored briefs** that make each conversation more productive.

### Brief A: The Pack Music Co-operative

**What we learned from web listening:**
- Co-operative model for local music streaming, Perth-based
- Working with Perth LGAs (Fremantle, Vincent, Subiaco) on Living Labs
- Lost original developers when company went into administration — rebuilt momentum with Bunya Fund support
- Crowdfunding for: tech development, artist onboarding, community outreach, Living Labs coordination
- Deliberately choosing Mastodon/Bluesky — values-driven platform selection
- Preparing for SXSW 2026

**Tailored conversation focus:**
The Pack already knows what they need. The conversation isn't about discovery — it's about fit:
1. What were the biggest coordination challenges during the developer loss and rebuild?
2. How do you coordinate across artists, businesses, LGAs, and fans? What tools do you use day-to-day?
3. Where does the coordination break down — is it tech, people, funding, or something else?
4. Would development support (e.g., FactoryK's build capacity) be useful as insurance against developer dependency?
5. Would CoachFlux-style ongoing support be relevant for your artist onboarding and community outreach?
6. What does your Living Labs coordination actually look like in practice — who does what?

**What to listen for:**
- Whether FactoryK's build pipeline could address their developer dependency risk
- Whether their LGA coordination model maps to Kamunity's campaign model
- Whether they've encountered specific digital coordination tools that work or don't
- Whether "co-operative" creates governance overhead that a platform could reduce

**Tier: Warm relationship — direct, specific, action-oriented conversation**

### Brief B: ALIKE WA

**What we learned from web listening:**
- Peak body for 780+ peer support groups in WA (40+ year history)
- Values: Accountability, Empowerment, Belonging, Humanity, Collaboration
- "Embracing new technologies" — not resistant, actively forward-looking
- Strategic Plan 2026-2029 just published
- Rebranded from ConnectGroups → Alike WA — modernising identity
- Membership is free; funded by WA government bodies

**Tailored conversation focus:**
ALIKE WA is a peak body, not a single group. The conversation should explore what they see across 780 groups:
1. Of the 780+ groups you serve, what are the most common coordination challenges you hear about?
2. Are there groups that coordinate really well digitally? What do they do?
3. Are there groups that struggle with digital tools? What does that look like?
4. Your strategic plan mentions embracing new technologies — what specifically are you exploring?
5. Do your groups need help finding each other and collaborating across groups? (Tests the nested groups / cross-group need)
6. What role does NP Digital Solutions-style needs mapping play for your member groups? Is that a service they'd value?

**What to listen for:**
- Whether the 780-group scale creates specific coordination needs (cross-group discovery, shared resources, collective advocacy)
- Whether their "new generation of peer support groups" has different digital expectations
- Whether they've evaluated platforms for cross-group coordination
- Whether the Volunteering WA INVOLVE platform is relevant to their groups (or whether peer support ≠ volunteering in their framing)

**Tier: Existing relationship — strategic, peak body perspective**

### Brief C: WALGA/LGA Contacts

**What we learned from web listening:**
- $900K cyber security pilot with Office of Digital Government for 5 LGAs
- Annual Aboriginal Engagement Forum (established protocols)
- 2025 Convention theme: "Lean into Legacy"
- Strategic Community Plan reviews underway in member LGAs (e.g., City of Perth)

**Tailored conversation focus:**
WALGA connects to member LGAs who connect to community groups. The conversation is about the institutional perspective:
1. What do your member LGAs hear from community groups about digital coordination challenges?
2. How has the cyber security pilot gone — what have the 5 LGAs learned?
3. Are there LGA community consultation processes that could inform our understanding of community needs? (e.g., Engage Perth)
4. Would a sovereignty calculator concept (helping communities understand SaaS dependency costs) be useful to LGAs supporting community groups?
5. Are there community houses or neighbourhood centres in WA that we should connect with?

**What to listen for:**
- Whether LGAs see themselves as digital capacity supporters for community groups (or not)
- Whether the cyber security pilot revealed community-level needs beyond institutional security
- Whether community consultation portal data is accessible and relevant
- Whether introductions to WA community/neighbourhood houses are possible

**Tier: Existing connections — institutional, bridge to grassroots**

| **Connect** | Pack Music's Living Labs concept could connect with Kamunity's campaign model | Explore in conversation — does the model map? |

| **Extend** | FactoryK could potentially address Pack Music's developer dependency risk | Explore in conversation — is this a service they'd value? |

### Triage Verdict (Updated)

The I03 verdict (Connect + Extend, no build) still holds for the listening package itself. But the web listening suggests that **when community conversations happen, some conversations may resolve at different Triage levels:**

- **Pack Music:** May resolve at Extend (FactoryK providing build support) or Integrate (connecting ecosystem components to their needs)
- **ALIKE WA:** May resolve at Connect (connecting 780 groups to existing cross-group tools) or at a needs discovery stage
- **WALGA:** May resolve at Connect (providing introductions, sharing findings from cyber pilot)

The Triage outcome will be different for each relationship. This is correct — Triage is per-need, not per-iteration.

## 4. What This Spec Does NOT Cover

- **Actual community conversations.** This remains a human action. The briefs prepare for it but don't substitute.
- **Grassroots/unorganised community voice.** Web listening cannot reach the digitally excluded, informal coordinators, or communities without web presence. The bias assessment in the raw research documents this honestly.
- **Aboriginal community engagement.** The AIATSIS boundary from I03 remains non-negotiable. WALGA's Aboriginal Engagement Forum is noted but is not a pathway for Kamunity engagement without appropriate protocols.
- **Technical evaluation of INVOLVE.** Identified as a Triage find but not evaluated. Should happen before any build recommendation involving volunteer management.
- **Accessing Volunteering WA's Barriers to Volunteering PDFs.** Identified as high-value Tier 1 sources. Should be accessed in the next iteration or by human review.
