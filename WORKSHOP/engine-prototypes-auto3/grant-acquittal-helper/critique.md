# Critique: grant-acquittal-helper

## 7 Constitutional Questions

### 1. Does something already exist that does this?
**Not for the target audience.** SurePact exists but is enterprise software with enterprise pricing. Free spreadsheet templates exist but require paid platforms (Smartsheet) or are static documents (DFAT Word template). No free, interactive tool for small community org grant acquittal.

### 2. Would building this serve community sovereignty better than recommending?
**Yes.** The only dedicated option (SurePact) is proprietary enterprise SaaS. A self-contained tool gives community orgs data control and zero cost. Grant financial data is sensitive — keeping it local is appropriate.

### 3. Is the scope appropriate?
**Yes.** The requirements describe a budget tracker with reporting output. This is:
- Enter data (forms)
- Calculate differences (arithmetic)
- Display results (dashboard + printable report)
- Export (CSV)
All achievable in a single-page app.

### 4. Could this cause harm?
**Low risk, with caveats.** The tool helps organize financial data — it doesn't make compliance decisions. Clear disclaimer needed: "This tool helps organize your acquittal data. It does not constitute financial advice. Verify requirements with your funder."

### 5. Am I following evidence or following the prediction?
**Following evidence — and contradicting the prediction.** My research found that (a) non-audited acquittals have no specific format, (b) the requirements are structurally simple, and (c) no free tool exists. The prediction's complexity claims are unsupported by evidence.

### 6. Would I have reached the same conclusion if no prediction existed?
**Yes.** The requirements are clear and scoped. The gap analysis shows no free tool. The Community Grants Hub confirms the task is simpler than it sounds. Without any prediction, I would assess this as a straightforward build.

### 7. Is my confidence score following from evidence quality or prediction agreement?
**Evidence quality.** My confidence is high because the requirements are clear, the gap is real, and the build is technically simple. I'm contradicting the prediction, so there's no inflation from agreement.
