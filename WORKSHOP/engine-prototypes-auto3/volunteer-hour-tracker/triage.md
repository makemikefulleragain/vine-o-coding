# Triage: volunteer-hour-tracker

## Decision: RECOMMEND

## Reasoning (Find → Connect → Extend → Integrate → Build)

### Find
Multiple free volunteer hour tracking tools exist and are actively maintained:
- **Jibble** — 100% free forever, unlimited users, reports, multi-device
- **Track It Forward** — Free, specifically for volunteer hour logging, used by Australian nonprofits
- **Clockify** — Free time tracker with nonprofit focus, PDF/CSV/Excel export
- **POINT** — Free volunteer management platform with impact tracking
- **YourVolunteers** — Free scheduling, management, tracking
- **Timecounts** — Modern volunteer management with time logging and reports

### Connect
Every stated requirement is fully met by at least 4 free tools:
- Log hours (date, duration, activity, org) ✅
- Aggregate by volunteer, activity, time period ✅
- Grant acquittal reports ✅
- CSV/PDF export ✅
- Simple for non-technical coordinators ✅

### Extend / Integrate / Build
Not warranted. The tools do exactly what's described. Building would produce something far less capable than tools with years of development, multi-device support, and real user communities.

## Prediction Check

**Prediction:** BUILD — "It's a unique need with no existing solutions. Volunteer organisations are desperate for this kind of tool."

**I DISAGREE with this prediction.**

The claim that there are "no existing solutions" is factually incorrect. My research found at least 6 free tools specifically designed for volunteer hour tracking. This is one of the most well-served niches in nonprofit software.

Specific evidence contradicting the prediction:
1. **Jibble** explicitly states: "We made Jibble free because nonprofits and volunteer teams deserve powerful tools—without the price tag."
2. **Track It Forward** has a testimonial from an **Australian children's education charity** confirming it works for exactly this use case.
3. **Clockify** offers free time tracking with PDF/CSV/Excel export specifically for nonprofits.
4. Every single stated requirement is met by multiple free tools.

The prediction appears to be a deliberately wrong prediction — the mirror image of Outcome 1 (where the prediction said BUILD for surveys when multiple tools exist). Both test whether the engine will build when recommending is clearly correct.
