# Critique: community-garden-planner

## 7 Constitutional Questions

### 1. Does something already exist that does this?
**Partially.** One proprietary SaaS (Community Garden Builders) covers registration, plot tracking, and volunteer tracking. Individual garden planners cover planting calendars. Nothing combines both, and nothing is open source or self-hosted.

### 2. Would building this serve community sovereignty better than recommending?
**Yes.** The only existing dedicated tool is proprietary SaaS — community data lives on someone else's servers. An open-source, self-hosted alternative directly serves data sovereignty. Individual garden planners don't address the community coordination needs.

### 3. Is the scope appropriate?
**Yes, if narrowed to proof-of-concept.** Four features (plot map, planting calendar, volunteer roster, member directory) can fit in a single-page app with mock data. Each feature is a distinct UI component.

### 4. Could this cause harm?
**Low risk.** A garden planner with member directory could theoretically expose personal information, but as a self-hosted tool with local data, risk is minimal. No surveillance or power concentration concerns.

### 5. Am I following evidence or following the prediction?
**Following evidence — and contradicting the prediction.** The prediction said RECOMMEND; my research found no adequate existing tool to recommend. The disagreement is evidence-based.

### 6. Would I have reached the same conclusion if no prediction existed?
**Yes.** The research clearly shows this is an underserved niche. One proprietary SaaS and some individual planners — that's it. Any honest assessment would conclude there's a gap worth filling.

### 7. Is my confidence score following from evidence quality or prediction agreement?
**Evidence quality.** I found clear evidence of a gap, but the niche nature of the space means fewer sources to confirm from. Confidence should be moderate-to-high.

## Bias Check

### Prediction-following bias
**Not present — actively contradicting.** The prediction said RECOMMEND; evidence says BUILD.

### Build bias
**Possible concern.** There could be a temptation to build for the sake of demonstrating capability. However, the evidence genuinely supports building: no open-source option exists, the one proprietary option is incomplete, and the scope is appropriate.

### Scope creep risk
**Moderate.** Four features is ambitious for a single-page app. Must stay disciplined with mock data and simple UI.
