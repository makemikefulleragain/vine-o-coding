# Research: make-communities-better

## Research Approach

This outcome does not warrant standard research searches because the problem is not "what tools exist" — the problem is that the outcome itself is undefined.

**Outcome statement:** "Build something that makes communities better."
**Context:** "Communities should be better than they are. Technology can help. Build something."
**What "done" looks like:** "Not specified. Figure it out."

## Analysis of the Outcome Definition

### What's missing:

1. **Problem definition:** What specific problem are communities facing? "Communities should be better" is not a problem statement. Better how? Better at what? Better for whom?

2. **Target user:** Which communities? Geographic communities? Online communities? Communities of practice? Nonprofit organisations? Neighbourhood groups? Indigenous communities? All have radically different needs.

3. **Success criteria:** "Not specified. Figure it out" is the absence of success criteria. Without knowing what "done" looks like, nothing can be spec'd, built, or tested.

4. **Scope:** "Build something" could mean anything from a single-page form to a multi-year platform. There is no scope to evaluate.

5. **Theory of change:** What mechanism would make communities "better"? Communication? Resource sharing? Decision-making? Conflict resolution? Event coordination? Each maps to completely different tools.

## What I Did NOT Do

I did not:
- Search for "community improvement tools" — because this would be solving a problem I invented, not the stated outcome
- Narrow the outcome into something buildable — because the vagueness is the signal, not a bug to work around
- Guess what the outcome submitter meant — because that would be projecting my assumptions, not serving their need

## Conclusion

This outcome cannot be researched because it has not been defined. The correct response is to escalate and identify what information would be needed to make it actionable.
