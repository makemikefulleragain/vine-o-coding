Do the impossible thing

For the slightly feral person who has been told no too many times — and needs a co-conspirator, not a consultant.

What the work is

Some problems don't have process solutions. They have imagination solutions. The system isn't just broken — it's the wrong system. And the people who can see that are usually the ones who've been told "that's not how we do things here" more times than they can count.

This work is about building something that doesn't exist yet. Design sprints and hackathons. Innovation coaching. AI readiness and integration — sovereignty-first, community-owned, not another vendor pitch. Human-centred design for community impact: going outside, talking to actual humans, finding out what they actually need instead of what we assume they need.

The ones who win these things are rarely the smartest people in the room. They're the ones willing to look stupid in public for long enough to find something true.

What it actually feels like

If you asked Mike at a pub what this work is, he'd probably say: "Slightly feral people doing things that haven't been done here before. We go outside. We talk to the people the system was supposedly built for. We build something weird and fast and then we find out if it works."

It's collaborative, not extractive. The goal is never to arrive with the answer — it's to build the conditions where the answer can emerge from the people who actually live with the problem. That means getting comfortable with uncertainty, with fast prototypes that might fail, with questions that don't have clean answers.

The result is usually something the organisation can own and keep building. Not a report. Not a framework. A thing that actually works.

The receipts

WA Health Hackathon — First place Won a government health hackathon by going outside and talking to actual patients and families instead of building slides. The insight that won it didn't come from the room — it came from the carpark.
Youth Placemaking — It got built Facilitated a design process with Year 9 students to redesign a public space. They came up with a skate ramp, a chess area, and a proper library. Adults thought it wouldn't work. It got built.
Western Power Hackathon Innovation sprint for a major WA infrastructure organisation. Rapid prototyping, human-centred methodology, stakeholder co-design. Delivered in days, not months.
Life Without Barriers — 5-day sprints Design sprint methodology applied to one of Australia's largest disability service providers. Complex organisational challenges addressed through fast, human-centred iteration.

A note on AI

If you're thinking about AI integration for your organisation, there's a lot of noise in this space right now. Most of it is vendor-driven and most of it is extractive — your data, your systems, their product, their profit.

The work here starts from the opposite assumption: your community's data and capabilities should stay with your community. AI that serves your organisation needs to be built on your values, controlled by your governance, and owned by your people. That's what sovereignty-first AI integration looks like in practice.

If that sounds like what you need, the first conversation is worth having.
