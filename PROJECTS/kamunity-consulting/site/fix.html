Door One

Fix the shit things

For the competent, slightly exhausted person who already knows what's broken — and needs someone who gets it.

What the work is

Most organisations in the community sector aren't broken because of bad people. They're broken because of accumulated drift — processes that made sense once, systems nobody reviewed, meetings that gradually replaced action. Everyone is working hard. The outcomes still aren't landing.

This work is about finding where the energy is being lost. QA audits and improvement planning. Process mapping and waste identification. Strategic alignment workshops. Team and stakeholder experience design. The kind of work that makes people say: "we should have done this years ago."

It's grounded in 30 years of quality systems — from the Deming/Juran tradition, applied to places Deming never imagined: disability services, community NFPs, local government, Aboriginal Community Controlled Organisations.

What it actually feels like

If you asked Mike at a pub what he does, he'd probably say something like: "Good people are carrying broken systems and it's killing them. I help find the three things that are causing 80% of the problem, we fix those, and everyone goes home feeling less like they're failing."

It's not a massive drama. Nobody needs to be blamed. The team usually already knows what's wrong — they just haven't had permission, time, or the right structure to say it. That's what changes.

The goal is that by the end, your team can name the improvement themselves. The capability stays with you. That's the point.

The receipts

RAC — $500k found in 9 months Process improvement engagement with one of WA's largest member organisations. Half a million dollars in efficiency gains identified and implemented. Nobody got fired. The team could name the improvement themselves by the end.
WALGA — Three engagements Strategic and process work across WA Local Government. Systems thinking applied to government-sector constraints. Sustained over multiple projects because it worked.
ALIKE WA — Strategy Strategic alignment work for the LGBTIQ+ community in Western Australia. Methodical, human-centred, grounded in real stakeholder experience — not a generic strategy template.

What you get

Every engagement is scoped to the actual problem — not a pre-packaged framework. But the consistent elements are: a clear diagnosis of what's actually broken (not what looks broken from the outside), a practical improvement plan your team can own, and facilitation that doesn't leave people feeling like they've been consultanted at.

Typical starting points: a QA audit, a process mapping session, or a "where are we actually?" strategic alignment day. If none of those sound right, that's fine — the first conversation is just a conversation.
